# Pawradise permanent game experience standard
Owner direction, 26 November 2026. Mandatory for every development batch, including maintenance, new screens, translations and nested interactions. This is a product acceptance specification, not a claim of implementation.

## 1. The browser is only the delivery mechanism
Pawradise must look and behave like a coherent, native-quality cozy Japanese-inspired life simulation. The owner’s visual references include illustrated miniature villages, isometric decorating games, a physical travel notebook, merchandise shelving, Animal Crossing-style human movement and a lived-in cafe with seated guests and a foreground counter. Learn from the spatial composition, interaction and visual hierarchy; do not copy assets, UI, brands or specific characters. Use original nostalgic Japanese watercolor/gouache artwork, warm timber, washi, subdued natural colors and consistent lighting. Cute, warm and playful for adults, not a generic pastel website or plastic toy aesthetic.

Every player-visible screen counts: entrance, district maps, rooms, gameplay, all dock tabs, every subtab, item detail, purchase confirmation, results, collections, settings, forms, error/empty/loading states, and share images. No plain web dashboard, generic cards, ornamental hero banner over a web form, floating tile grid, unrelated stock illustration, or repeated menu disguised with paper texture. An accessible HTML control may be used, but must be composed into the world and look appropriate. A beautiful background with web controls on top is not sufficient.

## 2. Scene construction and living inhabitants
Choose a consistent camera for each scene: angled/isometric cutaway for decorating and spatial exploration; intimate eye-level counter or tabletop for serving, tarot and other close-up actions. Do not force identical camera angles everywhere. Rooms are navigable places with floor and wall planes, doors, furniture with volume, shadows, correct scale, foreground and background occluders, and clear paths. Sparse starter rooms are intentional, not empty flat screens.
- Use a shared logical ground plane, projected foot anchors, walkable polygons, obstacle footprints and depth sorting. Route around furniture; do not cross walls, float on stairs, teleport through props, or place characters on unreadable signs. Camera and hit targets use the same coordinates. Use perspective-appropriate scaling only when physically justified.
- Front/back/side walking and turning must read as stepping, not sliding a sticker or bouncing the entire body. Match stride cadence to distance; stationary feet remain grounded. Preserve original human avatar identity and outfit across room and pose changes. Use authentic painted frame sequences and seated, kneeling, bowing, gripping, striking, resting and reaction poses where relevant; procedural substitutes are interim.
- Give each inhabited scene an appropriate resident or occasional visitor, with authored routines, walking, arrival/departure, seating, gestures, optional dialogue and quiet moments. A cafe can have guests already drinking tea, someone ordering and a resident leaving. Do not force a crowd or scripted greeting on every entry. NPCs must not block the avatar, door, activity or text. Vary appearances by session/day without pretending scripted NPCs are real humans. Preserve a consistent resident identity, voice and memory of relevant prior encounters.
- Use independent layers for furniture, doors, people, held objects, foreground and atmosphere. Props meet hands, feet meet floor, seated bodies meet seats. Interactivity must be functional, not baked into a decorative image.

## 3. Interaction contract: one purposeful tap
Prioritize walking and recognizable physical objects over menus of buttons. Tap a door to approach then enter automatically; tap an accessible activity prop to approach then begin or reveal a short contextual action; tap a resident for optional dialogue; tap a shelf item to inspect. Do not require a second confirmation for ordinary movement or entering a room. Confirm only consequential spending, destructive actions, privacy-sensitive publishing or irreversible choices. Provide an accessible alternative for fine motor gestures, clear focus order and keyboard/touch support.
- First tap feedback immediately (pressed state, start walking, sound or subtle motion); never silent delayed input. When loading, use short contextual world feedback with bounded fallback and retry, rather than a holding page or spinner covering the scene.
- Let the scene itself teach the action: counter, recipe ingredients, cabinet controls, table/deck, doorway, bell rope, garden gate, station platform. Brief action prompt only when the player is nearby or needs it; keep it away from hands, faces, signs and objects. No permanent labels hovering over every prop.
- Each nested screen must return to its immediate parent, restoring prior tab/filter/scroll/room position where appropriate. Closing an object interaction leaves the player at that object. Closing shop details returns to the same shelf. Closing room editor returns home, not map. Returning from a journal does not teleport the player. Preserve state, progress, rewards and saved data.
- Interdistrict train: physical platform, destination board, optional route selection then automatic boarding/arrival. Moving painted scenery distinctive to route, short journey and skip/reduced motion option. No extra arrival confirmation, cropped map as scenery or redundant permanent Next Stop overlay.

## 4. Navigation and naming authority
One feature, one canonical home. Keep four persistent dock destinations: Explore (探索), Today/Tasks (今日/小任务), Town Hall (小镇广场), My Room (小窝). Treat the precise short labels as proposed copy pending bilingual visual review; the four functions are mandatory. Explore contains chapter maps, passport/stamps, visited destinations and travel memories. Today contains optional tasks, daily luck and gratitude; lunar calendar is collapsible. Town Hall contains resident noticeboard, NPC chat and genuine shared lobby if configured; never show fake public messages. My Room contains the physical home, furnishings, collections, saved private readings/notes and decorating. Avatar/level/XP through top-left profile; Paw Coins visible in HUD; settings/language in profile, no redundant navigation. Keep world name Pawradise; no visible MeowBox, no repository/URL/save-key rename.

Use one canonical bilingual name per place, consistently across signage, map, dialogue, entry, activity results, collections and journey labels. A place name is a destination, not an instruction. Prefer evocative yet immediately understandable short names over generic repeated “House”, abstract invented labels or long descriptions. Keep existing IDs, links, assets and save mappings; only visible copy changes. Audit names across all current destinations before finalizing. Do not create a new place merely to rename an activity.

## 5. Physical interface by function
Travel = illustrated travel notebook with chapter tabs, stamps and page movement; use one readable page on small mobile and two-page spread where width allows. Tasks = handwritten planner; profile = resident identity card with actual full-body art and legible progress; shop = wooden shelves or showroom with physical item art and price tags; collection = drawers, display cabinet or album; room editor = actual angled furnished scene with compact tool tray only in edit mode; town board = pinned paper notices and resident identities; chat = speech bubbles beside the resident or a conversation space, with genuine public mode clearly distinguished; cooking/tea = work counter, ingredients and guests; arcade = real cabinet; tarot = cloth, cards, hand movement and readable result; settings = small coherent stationery panel. No unnecessary duplicate feature entry.

## 6. Release gates for every batch
Read this document before any UI or gameplay edits. Enumerate all affected click paths and nested states, including exits. Check naming consistency and duplicates. Test actual built game, not concept art: 360px and 390px portrait, tall phone, tablet, landscape and desktop; touch, mouse, keyboard; both languages; new/returning saves; slow loading, reduced motion, no-audio; actual scene screenshots before/during/after an activity. Review real foot contact, occlusion, props, sign readability, camera framing, back navigation, responsiveness and continued ambient life. Compare against the owner's visual references as a quality bar without copying them.
Reject any screen that still looks like a website, any invisible or unresponsive click, floating or clipping avatar/prop, unexplained duplicate menu, confusing label or misleading simulated community. Document exact commit, tested paths, screenshots and outstanding blockers. No completion or deployment claim based only on syntax or mockups. Group up to 20 related changes when requested; do not lower the acceptance standard or repeat completed work. Preserve current gameplay, repository URL, save keys, existing purchases and progress. Do not add new activities or locations to distract from unfinished core polish.

## 7. Resident communication and ambient life (owner clarification, 26 November)
Residents must sound like distinct people with their own lives, not tutorial dispensers or interchangeable joke generators. A conversation is optional, not the price of walking through a room. Most incidental encounters should be short autonomous statements in a nearby speech bubble, with no buttons, no requirement to acknowledge, and no interruption to movement. The bubble expires naturally (with accessible reading alternative), never covers faces, destinations, objects or controls, and does not pause a mini-game. If a player intentionally taps a resident, allow a natural greeting, specific topic or meaningful conversation; only show response choices where the choice changes the exchange, reveals a story, offers useful information, or supports a real activity. No mandatory OK, Continue, or generic affirmative choice for ambient speech.
- Context matters: speaker, district, activity stage, weather/time, earlier visits, relationship, last exchange and relevant town events. Give residents different vocabulary, rhythm, concerns and humor. Use natural English and independently adapted Chinese, not literal translations or generic affirmations. Personalize selectively, not by inserting the player's name into every line.
- Author short observations, overheard resident-to-resident exchanges, occasional multi-turn stories, activity-specific reactions, callbacks and quiet intervals. Example: Grandma notices a soup simmering; a railway passenger worries about a missed train; Auntie mentions a town event without always making a joke about work. Warm, ordinary lines are welcome. Do not make every character an office comedian or every line a question.
- Use stable authored line IDs and bounded per-resident/per-context recent-history memory; suppress exact repetitions and semantic variants of the same joke or sentence. Avoid repeat greetings each re-entry, immediate repeat lines after refresh, alternating two templates, and repeated auto-triggering while the player stays nearby. Use cooldowns, a session budget, and silence when appropriate. No artificial endless chatter, unsolicited popups, or speech queue backlog. Keep replay variation separate from skill/reward outcomes. NPC dialogue is fictional; never fabricate real community presence, user posts or AI responses.
- Verify bilingual dialogue length/meaning, intentional conversation branches, repeat visits, movement during ambient bubbles, proximity changes, switching rooms and tabs, and actual visual placement. Audit existing greetings, hints, activity reactions, NPC chat, cameos, notices, results, and all nested prompts before claiming completion. Ambient communication must not introduce another dock or mandatory interaction.

## 8. Mandatory scene direction, spatial contact and zero unintended overlap (27 November 2026)
Owner clarification: every scene must make physical and cinematic sense. The following defects block acceptance in every map, entrance, transit scene, room, activity, menu, nested screen and result.

- Readable composition: no unintended overlapping words, clipped text, speech covering faces, NPCs obscuring required signs, stacked buttons or controls covering the action. Check moving actors, camera positions and resizing, not only still screenshots. Prioritize essential information, reposition or suppress optional labels, preserve accessible alternatives, and check both languages. Intentional foreground occlusion is acceptable only if natural and not obstructing essential information or action.
- Grounded blocking: author entrance, walkable floor/path, approach, waiting, activity, exit and return anchors for each scene. Keep plausible position, orientation, perspective, size, foot contact, depth and shadows. No arbitrary central spawn, floating on scenery, walking through furniture, detached tools or teleporting during continuous interaction.
- Real illustrated poses: kneeling means the selected character visibly bends knees and rests on the mat/floor; bowing means the body actually bows; sitting means seat contact; drinking brings cup to lips; stirring, scooping, shaking, drawing and picking up show proper hand and prop contact. Preserve avatar identity and anatomical continuity; never imitate kneeling by lowering a standing sprite. Use original illustrated pose frames when required.
- Purposeful camera: establish the scene, follow the approach, zoom or reframe for the physical action, reveal the result, return to the same place. Close-ups for fine details such as cards, sticks, hands, incense and ritual poses; wider shots for walking and social gatherings. Keep the relevant body, prop and control visible. No arbitrary zoom, cropped action, abrupt cuts or unexpected hit-target movement. Compose phone, landscape and desktop independently; support reduced motion and contextual return.
- Verify through actual deployed normal-input play at entry, approach, pose/action, result, exit and repeat, including moving NPCs, language switch and interruption. Capture screenshots and animation evidence, fix and retest. Automated assertions assist but do not replace visual review. A label saying kneel or a successful callback is not proof of a kneeling pose. Preserve saves, stable IDs, the standing continue workflow and hard release gates.


## 9. Populated mixed-player town, natural exits and first-frame integrity (27 November 2026)

Every public district and public room must feel inhabited even when no real visitors are connected. My Room may be empty; private close-up activities remain private. Use computer-controlled travellers and residents with authored scene positions, seats, walking routes, entrances and departures, varied routines and meaningful optional interactions. Never fabricate human presence, online counts or posts attributed to humans. Real shared play must be opt-in, scoped to public areas, moderated, rate-limited and tested with independent clients. Preserve quiet solo play and private data.

Enable a meaningful daily social interaction with either actor type: approach, greet/wave, respond, converse or join a supported activity. A human peer must consent to interaction; NPCs provide an always-available path. Preserve existing task eligibility and reward caps; no compulsory human contact or reward farming.

All labels, exits and prompts must correspond to physical objects and actions. Inspect every room entrance, walk-in, approach, seating, departure and outside return point. Walk towards the actual exit, cross its threshold, then emerge outside the same building on safe ground, facing a logical direction. No arbitrary centre spawn or teleport within a continuous scene. Physical sign placement, legible live bilingual text and separate accessible hit targets take priority over decoration. Commit logical scene, coordinates, actors and visible render together only when the first frame is coherent.
