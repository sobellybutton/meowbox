## Common activity contract

1. Discover a clearly marked physical station; walk from the actual doorway along safe ground; stop facing the usable side of the item. Do not start before arrival or teleport into a new page. Use the selected avatar and correct host identity.
2. Author room-specific staging: entrance, exit, approach, standing/seated pivot, obstacle footprints, camera, interactable bounds, foreground masks, sprite/grip/seat anchors, safe zone for prompt and result. Match furniture perspective and actual painted art; no global scale multiplier. Record asset path and intended pose per beat.
3. Preserve full-body readability during entry and action (start by calibrating roughly 12–18% of usable mobile scene height for standing room exploration; use larger purposeful framing for action). Do not shrink people to fit everything on screen. Close-ups may show hands or over-shoulder view instead of a tiny full-body actor. Match NPC and player scale at equal depth; feet, hips and hands contact the correct surfaces. Test all selected avatar identities and facing directions; no floating tools, sliding stickers or clipped bodies.
4. Make the activity physically understandable: short contextual prompt, visible object, direct touch/drag/swipe/tap or accessible equivalent, anticipation, contact, result, reaction, recovery. Use responsive timing, distinct mechanics and outcome; animate the existing object at its anchor rather than drawing duplicate props. Keep art and simulation registered when resizing or switching language.
5. Keep the game in the room. Use a camera push-in, real tabletop, paper, notebook, chart, machine or counter as needed. Text entry can use native input within a designed diegetic surface; long results may use an in-world journal or readable paper layer only when necessary. No generic second webpage, duplicate Activities tab, giant Back to Game or language buttons, or nested menu to perform one action. A compact contextual back control returns to the same station; preserve in-progress state where sensible, and explicitly discard private unsaved input on exit where required. Language switch must preserve state. Keep 44px touch targets without making controls visually oversized.
6. Public rooms should feel inhabited with appropriate NPC arrival, movement, sitting, working, departures and short optional interactions; no cloned crowds, motion through props or compulsory greetings. Ambient movement continues when safe: steam, smoke, water, fabrics, lanterns, light, foliage, animals; asynchronous subtle loops, pause expensive offscreen work, respect reduced motion/mute. Empty private rooms are fine. Keep hosts only where their presence makes sense (Auntie need not sit at a private bottle/shout station).
7. Results should follow the actual action, be distinct, funny or reflective in natural EN/ZH, and stay in the same context. No false prediction, invented public messages or forced sharing. No duplicate rewards, repeated spending, stale callbacks, or loss of saved keepsakes. Replay/exit/cancel are understandable; meaningful variation without grinding.
8. Review the full scene and gameplay together: not just background quality. If an activity has no distinct purpose, use existing curated scope decision (improve, merge or retire with save migration); do not introduce new venues or revive removed fishing/bench games.

### Interaction design contract
- A station click begins the authored approach, stops at the correct host/object, faces it, and opens a short contextual conversation (or immediately starts a self-explanatory physical action). Give a simple greeting/question and a few relevant options within the existing scene, with a natural leave choice. Do not open a mode-selection page or redundant confirmation. More options may appear as a conversational follow-up only if needed, not a long nested menu.
- Preserve the world camera and selected avatar identity. Reframe smoothly toward the host/table, keep character and host visible at readable scale, use seated/standing contact poses and environmental movement. Dialogue may appear as compact speaker-linked text and accessible controls within the room, without obscuring actors, relevant objects or exits. Avoid generic giant text boxes as a substitute for scene composition.
