# Fortune Temple: Japanese Buddhist design acceptance contract
Approved direction: Japanese practice, 27 November 2026. This supersedes the earlier mixed Chinese/Japanese temple proposal. Fictional Pawradise temple inspired by Senso-ji; not a replica or a claim that every temple follows identical rituals.
Sources:
- Japan National Tourism Organization, Shrine and Temple Traditions: https://www.japan.travel/en/guide/shrine-and-temple-traditions/
- Senso-ji official FAQ, omikuji: https://www.senso-ji.jp/faq/
- Senso-ji official English site: https://www.senso-ji.jp/english/
- JNTO goshuin and ema: https://www.japan.travel/en/sg/story/collecting-sacred-memories-across-japans-shrines-temples-goshuin-traditi/

## Spatial flow
Main gate (sanmon) -> optional hand purification basin near entrance -> outdoor incense burner on central approach -> main Buddhist hall with visible Kannon-inspired altar and offering box -> omikuji counter beside hall -> paper tying rack near side path -> quiet garden, connected to same courtyard and exit. No compulsory ritual sequence, no new map or building needed. The garden is a visible side path, never a floating button or route through altar. Keep existing player state and prior activity records. Large temple bell is optional scenery; do not portray visitors striking it as universal daily etiquette. Japanese Buddhist prayer: bow, hands together silently, no shrine clapping. No Chinese joss-paper burning or moon-block confirmation in the Japanese route. Preserve legacy moon records and technical IDs for compatibility; do not present moon blocks as Japanese omikuji or require them to validate a draw. Any separate Chinese ritual experience requires a separate design decision, not another building by default.

## Omikuji interaction
Walk to counter; shake numbered-stick container, withdraw one stick (hand grip), return stick, open matching drawer, unfold corresponding paper. Stable draw result, not reroll on cancel/reopen; readable fortune text and number in selected language. Senso-ji ties bad fortunes at a designated place; player may optionally tie an unfavorable slip to rack, or keep a private record. No extra rewards for unlucky fortunes, no supernatural claims or coercive outcomes. Use a documented consistent Japanese rank set; rank list and probability distribution must match approved game rules rather than invented claims of temple tradition. Do not use Chinese 上上签/下下签 as the Japanese classification. Do not put mock fortune text in background art.
 
## Garden and visual asset requirements
Use Japanese Buddhist temple architecture, timber and tiled roofs, temple gate, accessible courtyard, quiet garden, no Shinto torii masquerading as Buddhist main gate. Replace abstract shrine niche with respectful visible altar artwork. Clean environment background in portrait and landscape with same architectural identity; separate handwashing basin, ladle, water, incense holder, smoke, candle glow, offering box, fortune tube/sticks, drawer cabinet, slip paper, tying rack and individual tied slips, benches, greenery, curtains, lanterns. Transparent character and object assets; hand anchor, foot anchor, depth order, obstacle footprint, approach points. Animate local smoke, water, leaves and cloth, not whole background. No baked text, ticks, labels, or currency values. Only selected EN or ZH text shown (Japanese cultural terms can be retained where relevant and explained).
 
## Acceptance before implementation and artwork
Mental UAT: first visit; skip optional activities; draw then cancel/reopen; tie result then revisit; no coins charged twice; return from garden to courtyard; reach exit without obstruction; rotate screen mid-ritual; slow or missing asset; English/Chinese; reduced motion; previous moon-block saves remain readable. Verify actual path geometry and art anchors against latest source before creating final plates. Test 360/390 portrait, tablet and desktop landscape with real gameplay recordings. This document is a design contract only. Existing runtime and art are not changed by this commit.
